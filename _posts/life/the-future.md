Well predicting the future is hard. And humans are very bad at it. We convince ourselves that we can do it because we think that if we logically play out the things of today, we can see the future more clearly. At best, the mental models we use to construct the future are bad. At worst, they are dangerous. Its almost like there is a veil hanging over the whatever events come in the next 5 years. Before the birth of aviation, controlled flight would have been scoffed at as a pipe dream. But in the 1940s and 50s, people imagined flying cars and travelling to other planets. After the launch of sputnik in 1957, and the start of the space race that culminated in the moon landing of 1969, people put their imagination on fast forward. The first general purpose digital computer, ENIAC was also created in the 1940s. This was then a time of optimism that the veil on the future that been lifted. It is hard to say what people might have been thinking at that time, but we can imagine it would have been of technological splendor - of machines and space exploration. Yet, people could not have predicted the birth of the personal computer (one that could fit the palm of your hand), the birth of the internet, of algorithmic content and machine learning. Of data driven biology, quantum computers or environmental degradation.

One thing that the people 100 years ago could definitely not have predicted is the massive explosion of information and the access to information. The amount of times 21st century humans resort to using technology that instantly connects them with each other and information in a day is stagerring. Think about what you did today? You woke up to some sort of smart device that calculated your sleep patterns to provide the optimal time to rise from slumber. You checked your phone to see if someone replied to your text or email or if your favorite group chat posted a new meme. You hooked on the algorithmic "kaiten" to be served the most fresh form of content. You tweeted about your lack luster experience at that fine dine restaurant to your 100 followers. You face-timed your grandmother, congratulating her on her acquisition of a new phone. The computing power in each person's hand today is unparalled to any time in history. And, tangentially, it is quite dissapoiting to see it being used to generate reels on instagram. But, I digress.

A massive failure of the 20th century predictors was to take what they saw in their everday lives and play it out in a linear fashion. They saw the planes and the space launches and the communication with satellites to think that there would be flying cars, cities on other planets and tv portals into new worlds. Sometimes I like to watch old sci-fi movies to see what people in the past thought the future would look like. Next time you watch 2001 the space odeyssey, place close attention to the shape of the computers, or the terms the characters use or the design challenge of the ui. You will se the thinking of the older humans reflect in their art. Maybe hearing this would make you feel disheartened that we are forever stuck to the confines of our material present. That thinking about the future is pointless because we cannot imagine one devoid of the current context of technology. And, I hate to break it you, that is quite the truth.

Maybe the better thing to do is not to predict the future, because we are so bad at it. Maybe it is to create the future that we wish to see in the world. I think there is a two pronged way to think about this. This is where the weekend half-assed research stops and the speculation begins. So bear with me for a second here. First prong of the approach is to identify what has not changed in the universe so to say over the last 100 or 200 years or so. On the larger scales, the universe is expanding and entropy is increasing. On the smallest scale, quantum mechanics has some rules for what can or cannot occur. At our energy scale, we see that humans generally follow some rules. We are social creatures and we tried to endow everything around us with meaning and symbolism. So this trend could continue indefinitely into the future, if we manage to survive. We could keep trying to find meaning in the universe and we could keep thinking about what it means to be a human, to be conscious, what the universe means. There would be new symbols for things and new forms of connection. But all the technology of the past, of today and of tomorrow will only reflect what has always been there - that we like to put labels and symbols to thigns, that we like to understand and create things, and that we wish to understand each other, ourselves and the universe.

The second pronged thing is the tools that will enlighten, help or degrade our desires mentioned in the first part. An example to illustrate the phenomenon would be considering the time of 1924 and 2024. What stayed the same between these two ages, well physics (the underlying theory of everything) didnt change, but our understanding of physics was illuminated through experiments and better models. The way humans found love, created communites and ostracized changed but the underlying mechanism for self-discovery,survival,self-attainment did not change. We still need food and shelter just as we needed back then. The tools to get the things we always needed have evolved. With the evolution of this technology, we now need to think in a different form about the underlying mechanism of human behavior, collective systems, the universe, consciousness. We are asking more or less the same questions, but the questions are getting more specific, better worded if you could say. The story of the universe is getting more clear.
